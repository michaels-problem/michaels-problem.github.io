A solution to Michael’s problem for Fréchet algebras

Antonio Acuaviva

This website accompanies the paper and its formalisation of Theorem A in Lean. The mathematical statement appears alongside its Lean formulation, with explanations of the definitions and hypotheses.

Theorem A

Let A be a commutative complex Arens–Michael algebra. Every character \chi\colon A\to\mathbb C is bounded on bounded subsets of A.

Here an Arens–Michael algebra is complete and Hausdorff, with a topology generated by submultiplicative seminorms. The algebra need not have an identity or be metrisable. A character is a nonzero complex-linear multiplicative functional; continuity is not assumed.

\forall B\subseteq A\text{ bounded},\quad\exists C\geq0\quad\forall a\in B,\ |\chi(a)|\leq C.

The bound may depend on the character and on the bounded set.

Read the Lean statement step by step

Read the hypotheses from left to right, then open the two definitions in the final line to see the character and the bound.

The square brackets are Lean's notation for implicit structures and compatibility conditions. They are still hypotheses quantified over by the theorem.
A may be the zero algebra. In that case there is no nonzero character, so the character clause is vacuous.
The result is boundedness on bounded sets. The continuity corollary for the metrisable case is separate.
The paper also gives the Fréchet-algebra consequence as Corollary B. The formalisation presented here proves Theorem A.
